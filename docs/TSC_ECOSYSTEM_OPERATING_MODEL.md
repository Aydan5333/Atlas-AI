# TSC Ecosystem Operating Model

## The idea

The Street Collective (TSC) is the shared home for creative lanes that can each stand on their own. Art, music, fashion, auto, media, education, and Atlas should each be able to make work, find an audience, earn revenue, and build proof without requiring every other lane to be active.

When lanes come together, they should make one another stronger and keep more of the value created by TSC work inside the TSC ecosystem. Atlas is the operating layer that helps the owner see the whole portfolio and coordinate shared work; it is not a replacement for each lane's identity or customer relationships.

## Operating rules

1. **Standalone by default.** Every lane has its own offer, audience, project pipeline, proof, and revenue path. A customer can hire one lane without buying into the whole brand.
2. **Connect when useful.** Join lanes when the work benefits from it and collaborators agree. For example, an artist-release project might combine original art, music, fashion imagery, and TSC-produced media. This is an option, not a requirement.
3. **Keep aligned work in the TSC ecosystem.** When TSC can deliver to its quality, schedule, safety, and compliance standards, prefer routing work through the appropriate TSC lane over sending spend to an unrelated brand. Bring in outside partners when they add needed skill, capacity, equipment, access, or compliance.
4. **Track contribution honestly.** Attribute the customer, scope, cash collected, direct costs, labor/time, collaborators, permissions, and proof to the lane and project that created them. Show both lane-level performance and a consolidated TSC view. Do not count an internal handoff as outside revenue or hide its real cost.
5. **Share infrastructure; preserve accountability.** TSC can share brand standards, content systems, tools, and administrative workflows. A shared dashboard provides visibility and coordination; it does not by itself create a new legal entity or combine anyone's accounts.
6. **Protect separate businesses.** Keep any partner- or family-owned company's books, contracts, insurance, customer obligations, and bank activity distinct from TSC, as the TSC operating plan specifies. For outside work supplied to TSC, document the actual scope, payer, costs, and agreement. Ask a qualified accountant or attorney about formal entity, tax, or related-party questions.
7. **Reinvest by evidence.** Prioritize booked-work obligations and reserves before funding new capacity or proof assets. Do not treat social attention as revenue, promise unsupported margins, or use preorder money as free operating cash.
8. **Earn the right to scale.** Expand a lane or add permanent infrastructure only after repeatable demand, delivery capacity, healthy contribution after direct costs, and customer proof are visible.

## Atlas implementation boundary

Atlas should eventually offer one TSC portfolio view with filters for lane, project, customer, and collaborator. Each lane should retain its own projects and work queue. Cross-lane work should be linked explicitly so the owner can see who contributed, what the customer paid, where costs occurred, and what proof can be reused with permission.

The current Atlas Creator Bridge is only a manual content/outcome log. The current local database is unencrypted, so do not enter bank credentials, account balances, tax records, payroll, or detailed financial statements into it. A proper multi-lane finance view requires a separate design decision covering privacy, storage, access, backups, and accounting export before implementation. The operating plan's spreadsheet remains the source for actual cash planning until that boundary is designed and tested.

## Example: one offer, several lanes

For a paid artist-release visual package, TSC Art could create original cover art, TSC Media could capture process and reveal footage, TSC Music could provide an approved excerpt, and Atlas could track the plan and manual outcomes. Each part should have an agreed scope, credit, permissions, cost, and owner. If the customer only wants cover art, the art lane should still be able to deliver independently.

## Measures to review

- Cash collected and direct contribution by lane and project.
- Shared costs and delivery time, with the basis for allocation documented.
- Paid inquiries, repeat work, referrals, and on-time delivery by lane.
- Cross-lane projects completed by agreement, including whether the combination improved the result.
- Outside spend that filled a real capability or compliance gap.
- Obligations, reserves, and capacity before expansion spending.

These measures support decisions; the current Atlas build does not calculate consolidated profit or automate finance.
